# Brand voice & content guidelines

## Purpose

Keep KeyNest sounding human and clear when talking about keys, access, and property.

## Personality in words

- **Say:** “Drop your keys at your nearest KeyNest Point.”
- **Not:** “Leverage our nationwide network of secure key repositories.”

## Writing rules (Brand Book)

1. Short sentences. One idea at a time.
2. Lead with the benefit: access, security, peace of mind.
3. Everyday words over technical or corporate language.
4. Friendly, not jokey — people’s keys and property are involved.

## UI copy patterns

| Context | Guidance | Example |
| ------- | -------- | ------- |
| Primary CTA | Verb + object | “Share access”, “Create report” |
| Empty state | Reassure + next step | “No keys shared yet. Add a guest to get started.” |
| Error | What happened + how to fix | “We couldn’t save. Check your connection and try again.” |
| Success | Confirm outcome | “Access shared. They’re notified.” |
| Labels | Nouns, sentence case | “Property address”, not “PROPERTY ADDRESS” in body (overline may uppercase) |

## When to use

Product UI, emails, in-app help, marketing body.

## When not to use

Legal/compliance text may need formal tone — keep structure clear but don’t force playfulness.

## Best practices

- Prefer “you / your” over “users”
- Avoid “please” spam; be direct
- Status chips: short adjectives (Active, Pending)

## Common mistakes

- Feature jargon in empty states
- Blaming the user in errors
- Long modal paragraphs

## Engineering notes

Centralise user-facing strings where i18n is planned; keep tone reviewable.

## Accessibility notes

Don’t rely on color alone in status text; include a text label.
